Commercial Freeze Drying Equipment

A commercial freeze dryer removes moisture from a frozen product under vacuum, a process called lyophilization. Because the water leaves as vapor without the product being heated to dry it, the finished piece generally keeps its shape, color, and much of its flavor and nutrient content. Removing the water also makes the product lightweight and shelf-stable when it is properly packaged.

Compared with hobby or countertop units, commercial and industrial equipment is built around larger chambers, repeatable cycles, and process control, so a production team can run the same recipe batch after batch. Which machine fits depends on the product, the batch size you need to move each week, and how the dryer fits into your facility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a commercial freeze dryer?

A commercial freeze dryer is a vacuum drying system built for repeated production runs. It freezes the product, then removes the water as vapor under vacuum. Commercial units differ from small home models in chamber size, process control, and duty cycle.

How does industrial freeze drying work?

The product is frozen, the chamber is placed under vacuum, and heat is applied gently so the ice turns directly to vapor (sublimation). A final drying stage removes remaining bound moisture. Cycle times and settings depend on the product.

How do I choose the right commercial freeze dryer?

Start with your product, the batch size you need, and how often you will run cycles. Then look at chamber capacity, vacuum and temperature control, automation, utilities available at your site, and support. Our team can walk through these with you.

What production capacity do I need?

It depends on your product moisture content, target weekly output, and how many cycles you can run. Share your expected volume in the quote form and we will help you compare models. For reference, the TSS-0123 has a 180 lb wet capacity.

What products can be freeze dried?

Many foods, pet foods and treats, and nutraceutical ingredients can be freeze dried. Results depend on the product and the cycle, so we recommend discussing your specific product before choosing a machine.
